How to Store Garlic

Whole garlic keeps for months with almost no effort. Peeled or minced garlic lasts days — and garlic in oil can grow Clostridium botulinum, which is why it must be refrigerated or frozen.

The botulism warning

Garlic is a low-acid vegetable grown in soil, so it can carry Clostridium botulinum spores. In oil — which has no oxygen and no acid — the spores can germinate and produce toxin.

Rules:

  • Homemade garlic in oil must be refrigerated and used within 4 days
  • Freeze it if you want to keep it longer — the toxin cannot form below freezing
  • Commercial garlic-in-oil is acidified and is safe at room temperature until opened
  • Never store homemade garlic oil in a cupboard

Botulism is rare but serious. This is one of the few food-storage rules where the risk is genuinely dangerous rather than just a quality issue.

How to store whole bulbs

  1. Cure home-grown garlic first. Hang it in a dry, airy place out of direct sun for 2–3 weeks until the skins are papery.
  2. Do not remove the papery skin — it is the barrier that keeps moisture out.
  3. Keep the stalk attached if you are plaiting or hanging.
  4. Store in a mesh bag, basket or clay garlic keeper — anything with airflow.
  5. Keep it dark. Light makes garlic sprout.

What not to do

  • Do not refrigerate whole bulbs. Fridge humidity makes them mouldy and sprout faster.
  • Do not use a sealed jar or plastic bag for whole garlic — no airflow means rot.
  • Do not store next to onions. Each makes the other sprout.
  • Do not buy pre-peeled garlic in bulk unless you will freeze it.

Freezing garlic (the best option for prepped garlic)

Freezing solves the botulism problem completely and keeps garlic for a year.

  • Whole peeled cloves — freeze on a tray, then bag. Use straight from frozen.
  • Minced with oil — freeze in an ice cube tray, then transfer the cubes to a bag. Each cube is roughly one clove.
  • Garlic paste — blend with a little oil and freeze flat in a bag so you can break off pieces.

Frozen garlic loses some bite. It is excellent for cooking and poor for raw use.

Is sprouted or green garlic safe?

Yes. A green shoot means the clove is using its stored energy — it will be milder and slightly bitter, but safe. Use it in cooked dishes.

Discard garlic that is soft, brown, mouldy, or smells sour.

Common questions

How long does garlic last?

Whole cured bulbs last 5-8 months in cool, dry, dark, ventilated conditions. Supermarket bulbs last 2-4 months. Peeled cloves last about a week in the fridge.

Should garlic be stored in the fridge?

No for whole bulbs - humidity makes them mouldy and sprout. Yes for peeled or minced garlic, and yes for homemade garlic in oil, which must be refrigerated and used within 4 days.

Is garlic in oil safe to keep in the cupboard?

No. Homemade garlic in oil can grow Clostridium botulinum because oil provides an oxygen-free, low-acid environment. Refrigerate and use within 4 days, or freeze it.

Why is my garlic sprouting?

Warmth and light. Keep bulbs below 10C in the dark with good airflow, and away from onions, which accelerate sprouting in both.
