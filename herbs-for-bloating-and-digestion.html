Herbs for Bloating and Digestion

Digestive herbs are among the best-supported in herbal medicine — but the most popular one, peppermint, makes reflux worse. Here is the split.

Peppermint: good for IBS, bad for reflux

Peppermint oil capsules have genuinely good evidence for IBS — one of the better-supported herbal treatments in gastroenterology. Enteric-coated capsules release in the intestine rather than the stomach.

But peppermint relaxes smooth muscle, including the valve at the top of the stomach. If you have reflux or heartburn, peppermint tea after dinner is likely to make it worse.

  • For IBS: enteric-coated peppermint oil capsules, 1–2 before meals
  • For reflux: avoid peppermint entirely

Fennel and anise

The traditional after-dinner digestive. Both contain anethole, which relaxes gut muscle and may reduce gas. Chewing the seeds or a tea both work.

  • Tea: 1 teaspoon of crushed seeds per cup, steeped 10 minutes
  • Chew half a teaspoon of seeds after a heavy meal

Ginger

The best-evidenced herb for nausea, including motion sickness and morning sickness. For general bloating the evidence is weaker but it speeds gastric emptying, which helps some people.

  • Fresh: 3–5 thin slices in hot water, 10 minutes
  • For nausea: 1–1.5 g of ginger daily
  • Avoid high doses with blood thinners

Chamomile

Mildly antispasmodic and very safe. Good for a nervous stomach — the gut and anxiety are tightly linked, and chamomile addresses both mildly.

  • 1 heaped teaspoon of dried flowers per cup, covered, 5–10 minutes
  • Avoid if allergic to other daisy-family plants

Bitter herbs

Gentian, dandelion root and artichoke leaf are traditionally used before meals to stimulate digestive secretions. The taste itself is part of the mechanism — bitter receptors in the mouth trigger a reflex. Artichoke leaf has some trial support for dyspepsia.

What matters more than any herb

  • Eating slowly. Swallowed air is a major cause of bloating.
  • Not drinking large volumes during meals — it dilutes acid and slows digestion
  • Identifying triggers. A two-week food diary beats guessing. Common culprits: dairy, wheat, beans, carbonated drinks, sugar alcohols (sorbitol, xylitol).
  • Walking after meals. Genuinely speeds gastric emptying.
  • Fermentable carbohydrates. If bloating is severe and daily, look up the low-FODMAP approach — it has the best evidence of any dietary intervention for IBS.

When to see a doctor

  • Unintentional weight loss
  • Blood in stool, or black stools
  • Persistent vomiting, or difficulty swallowing
  • Bloating that is constant rather than intermittent
  • A new change in bowel habit after age 50

Common questions

What is the best herb for bloating?

Fennel or anise seed tea after meals, and enteric-coated peppermint oil for IBS-type bloating. Avoid peppermint if you have reflux.

Does peppermint tea help digestion?

For IBS and general cramping, yes. For reflux and heartburn, no - it relaxes the valve and makes it worse.

How do you make fennel tea?

Crush 1 teaspoon of fennel seeds, pour on hot water, cover and steep 10 minutes. Drink after meals.

Can herbs cure IBS?

No. Peppermint oil has good evidence for reducing IBS symptoms, but it is symptom management, not a cure. Diet changes have stronger evidence.
