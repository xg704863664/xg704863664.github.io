Can You Freeze Yogurt, Milk and Butter?

Dairy freezes unevenly. Butter is unaffected, milk is fine for cooking but grainy to drink, and yogurt separates — though it can be rescued.

Yogurt: why it separates and how to fix it

Yogurt is a gel of milk proteins holding water. Freezing forms ice crystals that break the gel, and on thawing the water leaks out — you get a puddle on top and rubbery curds below.

To minimise it:

  • Freeze in small portions — smaller volumes freeze faster, making smaller crystals
  • Leave headroom — yogurt expands as it freezes and will push the lid off
  • Freeze flat in bags for quick thawing

To use it: thaw in the fridge overnight and stir vigorously. It will never be quite the same, but it is perfectly good in smoothies, curries, marinades, baking and frozen yogurt.

Do not refreeze it, and do not use thawed yogurt as a starter culture — the bacteria are damaged.

Milk: fine for cooking, poor for drinking

Whole milk separates because the fat globules clump during freezing. Shaking recombines it, but the texture stays slightly grainy and the taste is flatter.

Use thawed milk for:

  • Baking, sauces, soups, custard
  • Coffee and tea
  • Smoothies

Do not use it for drinking straight or for anything where milk is the point — a glass of milk, or a milk-based dessert.

Practical note: freeze milk in the amount you will use at once. Thawed milk should not be refrozen.

Butter: the easiest dairy to freeze

Butter is almost all fat, with very little water, so ice crystals have nothing to damage. It freezes and thaws with no change at all.

  • Keeps 6–9 months in a freezer bag or its original foil
  • Unsalted butter picks up freezer flavours faster than salted — wrap it well
  • You can grate frozen butter straight into pastry
  • It also keeps 3–4 months in the fridge, so freezing is mainly for stockpiling

Thawing rules

  • Always thaw in the fridge, never on the counter — the outside warms long before the middle
  • Never refreeze thawed dairy
  • Use thawed milk and yogurt within 3–5 days

Common questions

Can you freeze yogurt?

Yes, for 1-2 months. It separates because ice crystals break the protein gel, so stir it back together after thawing in the fridge. It will not be the same for eating plain, but is fine in smoothies, curries and baking.

Can you freeze milk?

Yes, for about 3 months. Whole milk separates and tastes flatter after thawing, so use it for cooking, baking or coffee rather than drinking. Leave headroom in the container because milk expands.

Can you freeze butter?

Yes - butter is the best dairy to freeze. It keeps 6-9 months with no texture or taste change because it contains almost no water for ice crystals to damage.

Can you freeze Greek yogurt?

Yes, 1-2 months, and it separates less visibly than regular yogurt because it is thicker. Stir well after thawing in the fridge.
