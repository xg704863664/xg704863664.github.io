How Many Chickens Should You Get?

The honest answer for a first flock is three or four. Here is why that number, and when it is wrong.

Why three or four

  • Chickens are flock animals. A single bird is stressed, noisy and often stops laying. Never keep one.
  • If one dies, two remain. With only two birds, losing one leaves a lone hen in exactly the state above.
  • Four gives you 3–4 eggs a day at peak — enough for a family without a glut.
  • Cleaning scales linearly. Eight birds means twice the bedding, twice the feed, and a much bigger coop.

A family of four eating two eggs each per day needs about 56 eggs a week — roughly 9–10 hybrid hens at peak. But production drops in winter and during moult, so most people keep fewer and buy the difference.

Space that number needs

  • Inside the coop: 2–3 sq ft per bird → four hens need about 1 m² of floor
  • In the run: 8–10 sq ft per bird → four hens need about 3.5 m²
  • Nest boxes: one per 4–5 hens is plenty; they queue for the same one anyway
  • Roost bar: 20–25 cm of bar per bird

Crowding is the single biggest cause of pecking, feather-pulling and disease. If you cannot provide the space, keep fewer birds — not more in the same coop.

When more than four makes sense

  • You want eggs year-round despite moulting and winter slowdowns
  • You plan to breed or sell eggs
  • You already have the space and the routine
  • You want meat birds as well as layers (keep them separate)

When fewer is better

  • Small urban garden with a small run
  • You travel often — a neighbour needs to cope
  • You are testing whether you actually like keeping chickens

Common questions

Can you keep just two chickens?

You can, but it is not ideal. If one dies the other is alone, and lone hens are stressed. Three is the practical minimum.

Do chickens need a rooster?

No. Hens lay without one. Roosters add noise and aggression, and many towns ban them.

How many chickens for a family of four?

Four to six gives you most of your eggs in summer and a surplus to give away. Ten would cover winter too, but needs much more space.

Can you add new chickens to an existing flock?

Yes, but introduce slowly and separately for 2-3 weeks. Sudden introductions cause fighting and sometimes deaths.
