How Often to Water Tomato Plants

Tomatoes want deep, infrequent watering, not a light sprinkle every day. Inconsistent moisture is the single biggest cause of split fruit and blossom end rot.

The schedule

SituationFrequencyAmount
In the ground, establishedEvery 2–3 days in heat; weekly in mild weatherDeep soak — 2–3 cm of water
In containersDaily, twice daily in a heatwaveUntil water runs from the bottom
Seedlings, just plantedDaily for the first weekSmall amounts, keep the root zone damp
After transplant shockDaily for 3–5 daysGentle, around the base
Cool, cloudy weatherSkip — check the soil first—

The rule that matters more than any schedule: water when the top 3–5 cm of soil is dry. Push a finger in; if it is damp at that depth, wait.

Water deeply, not often

Shallow daily watering keeps roots near the surface, where they dry out fastest and where the plant is most vulnerable. Deep watering pushes roots down, where moisture lasts longer and the plant is more drought-tolerant.

For ground-planted tomatoes, a slow soak twice a week beats a quick splash every day.

Water at the base, never the leaves

Wet foliage is how blight and fungal diseases get in. Water the soil around the stem, not the plant.

Water in the morning if you can — the plant takes up water before the heat, and any splashed leaves dry quickly. Evening watering leaves foliage wet overnight, which is exactly what fungi want.

Containers are different

Container tomatoes dry out far faster than ground ones. A 20-litre pot in full sun may need water twice a day in a heatwave.

  • Use the biggest pot you can — 30 litres or more. Small pots dry out in hours.
  • Mulch the surface to slow evaporation.
  • Check daily by weight — a light pot is a dry pot.
  • Do not let it sit in a saucer of water — roots rot.

Blossom end rot is a watering problem

Blossom end rot — a black, sunken patch on the bottom of the fruit — is caused by calcium deficiency, but not usually because the soil lacks calcium.

It happens when inconsistent watering stops calcium from being transported to the fruit. The plant cannot move calcium without a steady water flow.

The fix is consistency, not calcium tablets. Mulch, water deeply and regularly, and the problem usually stops on new fruit. Affected fruit will not recover — remove it.

Split fruit

Tomatoes split when they get a sudden large amount of water after a dry spell. The inside grows faster than the skin can stretch.

Fix: consistent moisture. In a drought followed by rain, water lightly beforehand so the plant is not shocked.

Mulch is the cheapest improvement

A 5 cm layer of straw, grass clippings or compost on the soil surface cuts evaporation dramatically, keeps the soil temperature steadier, and stops rain splashing soil onto the leaves.

It is the single highest-return thing you can do for tomato watering.

Common questions

How often should you water tomato plants?

Ground-planted tomatoes every 2-3 days in hot weather, or weekly in mild weather with a deep soak. Container tomatoes need water daily, sometimes twice a day in a heatwave.

Should you water tomatoes every day?

Not in the ground - deep, infrequent watering builds deeper roots. In containers, yes, because the soil volume is small and dries out fast.

Why are my tomatoes splitting?

Sudden heavy watering after a dry spell. The fruit expands faster than the skin can stretch. Keep moisture consistent and mulch to even out the swings.

Does blossom end rot mean my soil lacks calcium?

Usually not. It is caused by inconsistent watering preventing calcium from reaching the fruit, even when the soil has plenty. Fix the watering and mulch, not the calcium.
