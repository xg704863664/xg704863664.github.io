How to Keep Chickens Warm in Winter

The counterintuitive answer: do not heat the coop. Chickens tolerate cold well. What kills them is damp air and ammonia — both made worse by sealing a coop up.

Why sealing a coop is the wrong move

Chickens breathe out a surprising amount of moisture. In a sealed coop that moisture condenses on combs and wattles at night — which is exactly how frostbite happens, at temperatures well above what you would expect.

Ammonia from droppings builds up at the same time and irritates the respiratory tract. A sealed coop produces sick birds; a ventilated one produces cold but healthy birds.

The ventilation rule

  • Vents high, above head height — air leaves without blowing on the birds
  • About 0.1 m² of vent per bird as a starting point
  • Never blocked, even in a hard frost
  • No draught at roost level — that is different from ventilation and does cause harm

Should you heat the coop?

Generally no. Reasons against:

  • Fire risk — heat lamps are a leading cause of coop fires
  • Power cuts — birds acclimatised to warmth suffer badly when it fails
  • It masks the real problem — if the coop is damp, heat makes it worse, not better
  • Acclimatisation — birds that experience autumn cold handle winter far better

Exceptions: prolonged temperatures below about -15°C, or breeds with large single combs (Leghorns, Minorcas) in hard frost.

Water in freezing weather

Chickens drink as much in winter as summer, and a hen that cannot drink stops laying within days.

  • Bring water in at night, or use a heated base
  • Check twice daily in a hard frost — a thin film of ice is enough to stop them
  • Do not add sugar or salt to lower the freezing point; it is not effective at safe concentrations

Frostbite: what to look for

Pale, waxy comb tips that later turn black. Large-combed breeds and roosters are most at risk.

  • Do not rub or massage frozen tissue
  • Do not use hot water — warm gradually
  • Vaseline before a cold night prevents most cases

Moulting in winter

A hen that moults late goes into winter with thin feather cover. She needs 18–20% protein during regrowth — feathers are about 85% protein. A handful of sunflower seeds or a little meat helps.

Common questions

Do chickens need heat in winter?

Usually no. They tolerate cold well and are at more risk from damp, sealed coops. Heat lamps are also a common cause of coop fires.

At what temperature are chickens too cold?

Healthy, acclimatised birds handle -10C and below. Risk rises below about -15C, or for large-combed breeds in hard frost.

How do you keep chicken water from freezing?

Bring it in overnight, use a heated base, or check twice daily and replace it. A thin ice film is enough to stop them drinking.

Should you insulate a chicken coop?

Insulation helps less than ventilation. Prioritise high vents and a windbreak over insulation, and never seal the coop completely.
