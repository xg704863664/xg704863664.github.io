Herbs for Colds and Flu

Almost nothing cures a cold. A few things shorten it modestly if taken in the first 24 hours. Timing matters more than the remedy.

What has the best evidence

Zinc (the strongest single option)

Zinc lozenges started within 24 hours of symptoms can shorten a cold by roughly 1–2 days — among the better-supported interventions available.

  • Dose used in trials: 75 mg elemental zinc per day, as lozenges, spread through the day
  • Take with food to reduce nausea
  • Do not use nasal zinc sprays — they can cause permanent loss of smell
  • Stop after 5–7 days

Elderberry

Small trials suggest it may shorten influenza by a few days. Evidence is modest and some trials were industry-funded. Reasonable as supportive care.

  • Sambucus nigra extract, standard dose on the bottle
  • Must be cooked — raw elderberries are toxic
  • Avoid with autoimmune conditions or immunosuppressants

Echinacea

Mixed results. Some trials show a modest reduction in duration; others show nothing. Different species and preparations make comparison hard. Low risk, uncertain benefit.

Vitamin C

The honest answer: it does not prevent colds in the general population, and shortens them by less than a day, if at all. It is cheap and safe, but it is not the answer people think it is.

Honey

For cough and sore throat, honey has beaten a common cough medicine in trials. One to two teaspoons. Not for babies under one year.

What helps symptoms

  • Steam and saline — a saline nasal rinse genuinely reduces congestion and duration slightly
  • Thyme and marshmallow root — see our sore throat guide
  • Sleep — the single most underrated intervention. Sleep-deprived volunteers catch colds more easily in controlled studies.
  • Fluids — comfort, not cure, but dehydration makes everything worse

What does not work

  • Antibiotics — useless against viruses, and harmful in overuse
  • Colloidal silver — no evidence, and it can permanently discolour skin
  • High-dose vitamin C megadoses — diarrhoea, no benefit
  • Essential oils swallowed — dangerous

See a doctor if

  • Shortness of breath or chest pain
  • Fever above 39°C / 102°F, or lasting more than 4 days
  • Symptoms that improve then suddenly worsen
  • Severe headache with a stiff neck
  • You are pregnant, elderly, or immunocompromised

Common questions

What is the fastest way to get rid of a cold?

Nothing cures it, but zinc lozenges started within 24 hours can shorten it by 1-2 days. Sleep and fluids matter more than most people expect.

Does vitamin C prevent colds?

No, not in the general population. Regular supplementation shortens colds by less than a day, if at all.

Does elderberry work for flu?

Small trials suggest it may shorten influenza by a few days, but the evidence is modest and some trials were industry-funded.

How much zinc should you take for a cold?

Trials used about 75mg of elemental zinc per day as lozenges, started within 24 hours, for 5-7 days. Take with food to reduce nausea.
