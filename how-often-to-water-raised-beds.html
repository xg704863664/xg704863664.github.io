How Often to Water a Raised Bed

A raised bed dries out roughly twice as fast as the ground beside it — more surface area, more airflow, and usually better drainage. Watering on a fixed schedule is why so many raised beds fail.

Start here: the 10-second test

Push a finger in up to the second knuckle, about 5 cm / 2 in down. If it is dry at that depth, water. If it is damp, wait. This beats every calendar rule, because sun, wind and bed size vary enormously.

A cheap moisture meter works too, but the finger test is more reliable in a raised bed because the top surface dries misleadingly fast.

How much water

  • Goal: about 2.5 cm / 1 in of water per week, including rain
  • Per plant in a raised bed: roughly 1–2 litres for a tomato or pepper in summer, more in a small bed
  • Water deeply, less often. Shallow daily sprinkling grows shallow roots that then fail in a heatwave
  • Water the soil, not the leaves. Wet foliage spreads blight and mildew

Why raised beds dry out faster

  • Air on all four sides — the walls wick and evaporate moisture
  • Shallow soil — a 15 cm bed holds half the water of a 30 cm one
  • Good drainage — great for roots, but it means water leaves quickly
  • Dark or metal walls heat up and cook the root zone

Five changes that cut watering in half

  1. Mulch 5 cm deep — straw, compost or grass clippings. This is the single biggest win.
  2. Deeper beds — 30 cm holds far more than 15 cm.
  3. Soak, don't sprinkle — a soaker hose or a can emptied slowly at the base.
  4. Group thirsty crops together — tomatoes, cucumbers and squash in one zone; herbs and beans in another.
  5. Shade cloth in a heatwave — 30% shade cuts water loss dramatically.

Crops that tell you loudly

  • Tomatoes: curled leaves and blossom-end rot mean inconsistent water
  • Lettuce: bolts to seed almost overnight when stressed
  • Squash: wilts in the afternoon even when the soil is damp — that is usually heat, not thirst
  • Herbs: rosemary, thyme and oregano prefer it dry; overwatering kills more of them than drought

Common questions

Should I water a raised bed every day?

Only in peak summer heat, or in a shallow bed. Otherwise every 2-3 days with a deep soak is better for root development.

Morning or evening?

Morning. It lets foliage dry through the day and reduces fungal disease. Evening watering leaves leaves wet overnight.

How do I know if I am overwatering?

Yellowing lower leaves, soggy soil, fungus gnats and wilting despite damp soil. If the finger test says damp, do not water.

Does a raised bed need more water than the ground?

Yes, usually about twice as often, because of the exposed sides and better drainage.
