Backyard Chickens for Beginners: The Complete First Year

Most beginner problems with backyard chickens are decided before the birds arrive. This is the whole first year in order - setup, feeding, laying, winter and the problems you will actually meet.

Before you buy: the four decisions

1. How many birds

Three or four. Chickens are flock animals - a single bird is stressed and noisy. More than four means a lot of cleaning in your first year.

2. Coop size

Roughly 2-3 sq ft per bird inside and 8-10 sq ft in the run. Crowding is the single biggest cause of pecking, feather-pulling and disease.

3. Ventilation, not warmth

A sealed coop holds ammonia and moisture and causes respiratory disease. Leave high vents open all winter - chickens tolerate cold far better than damp.

4. Local rules

Check your city or HOA rules first. Many areas allow hens but not roosters, and some set minimum distances from neighbouring houses.

Weeks 1-2: settling in

Keep new birds inside the coop and run for the first week so they learn where home is. Expect a drop in laying while they settle. Handle them daily - it makes everything later much easier.

Weeks 3-4: the routine

  • Layers pellets, roughly 1/4 lb (110-120g) per bird per day, free-fed
  • Grit available separately for digestion
  • Oyster shell separately for calcium and strong shells
  • Clean water, checked daily
  • Eggs collected daily - leaving them encourages egg-eating

Weeks 5-8: the first real problems

Pecking

Almost always space or boredom. Increase run size, add things to peck at, and isolate a bird that is being badly targeted.

Red mite

Grey specks that turn red when fed. They hide in coop crevices by day and feed at night. Clean the coop with a miticide and repeat after a week to catch hatching eggs.

Muddy run

Mud causes foot problems. Bark chip, a roof over part of the run, or moving the run periodically all help.

Laying: what to expect

Most breeds start at 18-22 weeks. You do not need a rooster. Expect a slow-down or stop through the first winter and during the annual moult - that is normal, not a problem.

Winter care

  • Keep ventilation open, block only direct draughts
  • Insulate drinking water or check it twice a day
  • Feed slightly more in cold spells - they burn energy staying warm
  • Watch for frostbite on combs in hard frosts

What it actually costs

Feed, bedding and grit run roughly $20-40 per bird per year. The coop and run are the big one-off cost. Home eggs are not usually cheaper than shop eggs - the gain is quality, freshness and knowing what the birds ate.

Common questions

How many chickens should a beginner start with?

Three or four. A single bird is stressed; more than four is a lot of cleaning in year one.

How much space do backyard chickens need?

2-3 sq ft per bird inside the coop, 8-10 sq ft in the run.

How long until chickens start laying?

18-22 weeks for most breeds. Fewer or no eggs through the first winter and the moult.

Do you need a rooster for hens to lay eggs?

No. Hens lay without one - a rooster is only needed for fertilised eggs.
