Natural Cough Remedy for Adults: What Actually Helps

Most shop-bought cough syrups perform barely better than placebo in trials. These are the remedies with actual evidence behind them, with the amounts and preparation details that matter.

1. Honey — the strongest single option

Honey is the only home remedy for cough that reliably beats a placebo in trials, and it outperformed dextromethorphan in at least two studies of night-time cough in adults and children over one year.

Dose: 1–2 teaspoons (5–10 ml) straight, or stirred into warm — not boiling — water. Take it at bedtime, when cough is most disruptive.

Do not: give honey to a child under one year (infant botulism risk), or use it if you are diabetic without accounting for the sugar.

2. Thyme infusion

Thyme contains thymol, which is why it appears in commercial cough preparations. A home infusion is weaker than a standardised extract but pleasant and low-risk.

Method: 1 teaspoon dried thyme (or 3 sprigs fresh) per cup. Pour boiling water over, cover, steep 10 minutes, strain.

Dose: up to 3 cups a day. Add honey once it has cooled enough to drink.

Caution: thyme is a mild uterine stimulant — avoid medicinal doses in pregnancy. Culinary amounts are fine.

3. Marshmallow root cold infusion

Marshmallow root soothes a raw, dry cough by coating the throat with mucilage. The important detail: mucilage is destroyed by heat, so a normal tea does not work.

Method: 1 tablespoon dried root in 250 ml cold water. Leave 4–8 hours or overnight, then strain.

Dose: 2–3 cups a day, sipped slowly.

Note: take it at least an hour away from other medications — the mucilage can slow absorption.

4. Salt water gargle

Half a teaspoon of salt in a glass of warm water, gargled for 30 seconds, several times a day. It reduces the throat irritation that triggers the cough reflex — it does not treat the cough itself.

5. Steam and humidity

Dry air makes a dry cough worse. A bowl of hot water with a towel over your head for 5–10 minutes, or simply a humidifier in the bedroom, is cheap and helps at night.

Do not add essential oils to steam inhalation for children, and never use menthol products on the face of an infant.

What does not work

  • Over-the-counter cough syrups — most contain dextromethorphan or guaifenesin, both of which have repeatedly failed to beat placebo in trials
  • Vitamin C after symptoms start — no effect
  • Antibiotics — useless for viral coughs, which are the vast majority
  • Echinacea — inconsistent results, and no clear effect on cough

When to see a doctor

  • Cough lasting more than 3 weeks
  • Coughing up blood or rust-coloured sputum
  • Breathlessness at rest, or chest pain
  • Fever above 38.5°C lasting more than 3 days
  • Any cough in someone immunocompromised, or with COPD or asthma

Common questions

What is the best natural remedy for a cough?

Honey. It is the only common home remedy that reliably beats placebo in trials, and it outperformed dextromethorphan for night-time cough in several studies. One to two teaspoons at bedtime.

Does honey really work for a cough?

Yes - it is the best-supported home option. Give 5-10 ml at bedtime. Never give honey to a child under one year because of infant botulism risk.

How do you make marshmallow root tea for a cough?

Use cold water, not hot. One tablespoon of dried root in 250 ml cold water, left 4-8 hours, then strained. Heat destroys the mucilage that does the soothing.

How long should a cough last?

A viral cough typically settles in 3 weeks. Beyond that, or with blood, breathlessness or chest pain, see a doctor.
