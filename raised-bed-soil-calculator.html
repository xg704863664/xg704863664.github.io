How the calculation works

Volume = length × width × depth, all in the same unit. That is the whole formula — the calculator just does the conversions and rounds the bag count up.

Soil mix ratios

ComponentShareWhy
Topsoil or loam60%Body and minerals; holds the structure
Compost30%Nutrients and water retention
Coarse sand or perlite10%Drainage; prevents compaction

For herbs and Mediterranean plants, push the sand/perlite share to 20%. For hungry crops like tomatoes and squash, go 50% topsoil and 40% compost.

What to put in the bottom

  • Cardboard — one layer, to smother weeds. It breaks down within a season.
  • Not plastic or landscape fabric — it creates a saturated layer and blocks worms.
  • Not gravel — it does not improve drainage in a raised bed; it just raises the water table inside the bed.
  • Logs and branches — a hugelkultur base works, but expect the soil level to drop as they rot. Fill to full depth anyway.

Cheaper ways to fill a deep bed

A 40 cm bed needs a lot of bought soil. Two options that work:

  1. Fill the bottom third with organic matter — logs, leaves, grass clippings, kitchen scraps. Top the last 25 cm with proper mix.
  2. Build shallower — a 25 cm bed costs about 40% less to fill and grows most vegetables fine.

Common questions

How much soil does a 4x8 raised bed need?

A 4x8 ft bed at 12 inches deep needs about 32 cubic feet, or roughly 0.9 cubic metres — around 23 bags of 40 litres.

How many bags of soil for a raised bed?

Divide the volume in litres by the bag size and round up. A 1.2 x 2.4 m bed at 30 cm needs about 864 litres, so 22 bags of 40 litres.

Should I fill a raised bed with only compost?

No. Pure compost compacts, holds too much water and is expensive. Mix it with topsoil and a drainage component.

How deep should the soil be?

30 cm suits most vegetables. 15 cm works for lettuce and herbs; 40 cm or more for carrots, tomatoes and potatoes.
