Raised Bed Gardening in a Small Yard

Raised beds solve the two biggest small-yard problems: bad soil and bad drainage. Here is how to size, place and fill them without wasting money on soil you do not need.

Why raised beds work in small spaces

  • You control the soil. No digging, no amending clay, no competing with tree roots.
  • Better drainage. Water moves down and out instead of pooling.
  • Warmer in spring. Soil in a raised bed warms 1–2 weeks earlier than ground soil.
  • Less bending. A 45–60 cm (18–24 in) height means far less stooping.
  • Fewer slugs. A defined edge and drier surface helps.

Never make a bed wider than 1.2 m. If you cannot reach the middle, you will step into the bed, which compacts the soil you went to the trouble of building.

Where to put it

Six hours of direct sun is the practical minimum for vegetables. Fruiting crops — tomatoes, peppers, courgettes — want eight.

Other placement rules:

  • Long axis east–west so plants do not shade each other
  • Near a water source — you will water more than you expect in the first year
  • Not under a tree — roots steal water and nutrients, and the canopy casts shade
  • On the flattest ground you have — a sloping bed erodes and dries unevenly

How much soil you need

Volume = length × width × depth. For a 2.4 m × 1.2 m bed at 30 cm deep:

2.4 × 1.2 × 0.3 = 0.86 m³ — roughly 860 litres, or about 30 bags of 30 L.

That is the single most common budgeting mistake: a raised bed takes far more soil than people expect. Use the soil calculator to get the exact figure including the bag count.

What to fill it with

You do not need to fill the whole depth with bought topsoil.

  • Bottom third: logs, branches, cardboard, grass clippings — free filler that rots down over a season (hügelkultur)
  • Middle: garden soil, compost, aged manure
  • Top 15 cm: your best compost — this is where seeds germinate and roots feed

A good general mix is roughly 60% topsoil, 30% compost, 10% sand or perlite for drainage.

Common questions

How deep should a raised bed be?

25 cm (10 in) is the practical minimum for most vegetables. Use 45 cm (18 in) if you want to avoid bending, or for deep-rooted crops like carrots and parsnips.

What is the best width for a raised bed?

1.2 m (4 ft) maximum, so you can reach the centre from either side without stepping on the soil. Beds against a wall should be 60 cm wide.

Do raised beds need a bottom?

No. Leave the bottom open to the ground so worms and roots can move freely, and water can drain. Only line the base with cardboard if you are smothering grass underneath.

How much soil does a raised bed need?

Multiply length x width x depth. A 2.4 m x 1.2 m bed at 30 cm deep needs about 0.86 cubic metres - roughly 860 litres, or 30 bags of 30 litres.
